# A Performance–Robustness Tradeoff in Connectome-Derived Neural Representations

## Abstract

How biological recurrent connectivity shapes the geometry and robustness of neural representations remains unclear. We use the chemical-synapse connectome of 297 *C. elegans* neurons, with contact-count weights and predicted synaptic polarities where available, as the fixed recurrent matrix of an echo-state network. We characterize reservoir state-space geometry using effective-rank measures: kernel rank quantifies the separation of distinct input histories, while generalization rank quantifies sensitivity to earlier history after receiving a common recent input sequence. We complement these with memory capacity and truncated single-delay information-processing capacity. To identify architectural contributions, we vary excitatory/inhibitory edge balance and construct controlled perturbations of connectivity, sign placement, weight magnitude, and weight placement. Each condition is evaluated over 96 combinations of target spectral radius, leak rate, input scaling, and neuronal bias, with 200 trials per condition; robustness is quantified by the coefficient of variation across this grid. The empirical connectome occupies a relatively low-variance rather than peak-performance regime. Across excitatory/inhibitory-balance sweeps and shuffle controls, architectures with greater memory, nonlinear capacity, and state-space separation also tend to be more hyperparameter-sensitive and retain more variation from earlier input history, indicating poorer generalization. This performance–robustness tradeoff covaries with raw spectral radius before normalization: matrices with smaller raw radius are amplified more strongly when rescaled to a common target, coupling structural perturbations to recurrent-weight scale. Thus, rather than maximizing representational dimensionality, the connectome occupies a stable operating regime shaped jointly by topology, edge signs and magnitudes, and architecture-dependent rescaling under spectral-radius normalization.

## TL;DR

A *C. elegans* connectome reservoir occupies a stable, submaximal-performance regime: greater representational separation trades off against robustness and generalization while covarying with architecture-dependent spectral rescaling.

## Relevance to NeurReps

This work studies how a signed, weighted biological connectome, viewed as a recurrent dynamical graph, shapes the dimensionality, separability, and robustness of neural state representations. By perturbing topology, synaptic signs, and weight placement and analyzing effective rank across dynamical regimes, it connects computational neuroscience with NeurReps themes of neural representation dynamics, graph structure, and dynamical systems. The results show how spectral-radius normalization couples architecture to recurrent-weight scale and representational geometry, clarifying a performance–robustness tradeoff in biological and artificial recurrent networks.
