This work fits NeurReps by examining how recurrent connectivity shapes the geometry, effective dimensionality, and robustness of neural state spaces. Using the C. elegans chemical-synapse connectome as a reservoir, we perturb topology, excitatory/inhibitory edge balance, and the magnitudes and placement of weights, then assess memory, nonlinear processing, kernel rank, and generalization rank. Across these variants, greater memory and processing capacity, together with stronger state-space separation, tend to coincide with higher hyperparameter sensitivity and poorer generalization across input histories. Furthermore, the empirical C. elegans network lies on the lower-variance side of this tradeoff, while comparisons among architectures with the same edge balance show that topology, sign placement, and weight structure further distinguish performance and robustness. We show that raw spectral radius covaries with these effects, highlighting how the architecture-dependent rescaling needed to reach a common target radius can complicate comparisons between recurrent networks. Together, these findings reveal how biologically grounded connectivity influences the information encoded by recurrent dynamics, directly addressing NeurReps’ focus on the organization of neural representations.
